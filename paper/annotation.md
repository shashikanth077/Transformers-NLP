This project paper studies the Transformer architecture and its application to natural language processing (NLP) tasks. The theoretical part explains how self-attention, multi-head attention, positional encoding and large-scale pre-training replaced recurrent neural networks as the dominant approach in NLP, and reviews the main families of Transformer models (encoder-only BERT-type models, decoder-only GPT-type models and encoder–decoder models such as T5 and BART). The literature review covers international research and research carried out in Lithuania, including work on Lithuanian sentiment analysis, the trilingual LitLat BERT model and the first open Lithuanian large language models. It concludes that most published comparisons are done on GPU servers with large models, while evidence on what Transformers offer under realistic resource limits (a laptop CPU, a few thousand labelled examples) is scarce.

The practical part answers this question experimentally on binary sentiment classification of movie-review sentences (Stanford Sentiment Treebank, SST-2). Ten models from four families were implemented in Python (PyTorch, Hugging Face Transformers, scikit-learn) and compared under one protocol: three classical TF-IDF models, a bidirectional LSTM trained from scratch, three Transformer encoders that we fine-tuned ourselves on a CPU (BERT-tiny, BERT-small and DistilBERT), and three ready-made Transformer models used without any training (an in-domain DistilBERT, an out-of-domain Twitter RoBERTa and a zero-shot natural language inference model). Models were evaluated by accuracy, macro F1, training time, inference latency and size, as well as on linguistically difficult subsets with negation and contrast. A learning-curve experiment measured how much labelled data each approach needs. DistilBERT fine-tuned on only 8,000 sentences reached 88.8% accuracy and significantly outperformed the best classical model trained on all 65,349 sentences (82.9%), while the ready-made in-domain DistilBERT reached 91.1%. Compact Transformers only matched the classical models, and the recurrent network without pre-training gave no advantage. Pre-training made Transformers far more data-efficient (+14.1 percentage points with 500 labelled sentences) and more robust on negation, at the cost of 20–2,000 times slower inference on a CPU. Practical recommendations for choosing a model are given.

**Keywords:** Transformer, self-attention, BERT, DistilBERT, fine-tuning, transfer learning, sentiment analysis, natural language processing.
