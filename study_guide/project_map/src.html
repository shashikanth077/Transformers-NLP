Part 3

Interview and viva preparation

The 30-second pitch

We studied the Transformer architecture and tested, on a laptop without a GPU, whether Transformers really beat older NLP methods. We compared ten sentiment classifiers (TF-IDF models, a BiLSTM, Transformers we fine-tuned, and ready-made ones) on SST-2. Result: a Transformer fine-tuned on only 8,000 sentences reached 88.8% against 82.9% for the best classical model trained on all 65,349, but it is 20 to 2,000 times slower. So the answer is: use a pre-trained Transformer when quality matters and the cost is acceptable.

The 2-minute story (say it in this order)
  1. Problem and why sentiment (common task, hard cases: negation, contrast).
  2. Five research questions: accuracy, cost, data efficiency, ready-made models, hard sentences.
  3. Ten models in four families; one dataset, one laptop, one seed.
  4. Quality, then cost, then data efficiency, then robustness.
  5. Statistics: confidence intervals and McNemar test.
  6. Recommendations and honest limitations.

Part 4 · Why we did this

Our purpose

To understand the Transformer properly, and to find out with honest measurements, on ordinary hardware, when a Transformer is worth its cost for a real NLP task. Then to turn what we learned into material that other students can use, in simple English and in Kannada.

Explain it. Measure it. Make it useful for everyone.
Explain itA paper, a talk, interactive guides, diagrams and code walkthroughs: from tokens and attention to encoder-decoder translation, RNNs and CNNs.
Measure itTen models, one fair set-up, confidence intervals and paired tests, cost as well as accuracy, and the limits stated openly.
Make it usefulPractical advice (reuse a public model first, fine-tune a distilled one next) and learning notes in Kannada so the ideas reach people in their own language.

What we learned

  • Pre-training is the real advantage: it gives accuracy and data efficiency.
  • Bigger is not automatically better: tiny Transformers only matched or lost to classical models.
  • Cost matters: the best model is not always the right model.
  • A result without a confidence interval is an anecdote.

What we would do next

  • Repeat runs with several random seeds and report the spread.
  • Measure latency on a GPU as well as a CPU.
  • Apply the same method to Lithuanian (LitLat BERT, XLM-RoBERTa) and to Kannada text.
  • Extend from classification to translation and generation.
